# Daily AI Brief — Image Specification for an External App

**Revision:** 2026-10-09, aligned with external-app prompt revision 4.  
**Operational repository:** `gttome/Daily-AI-Brief-Compiler`.  
**Scope:** image quality and review for an existing app that reads the prompt and GitHub, then returns six PNGs to GitHub. No Work chat, fresh-chat proof, specific provider or app build is required.

This replaces the operational instructions in the uploaded October 7 specification. That document summarized the production repository at `081d9f635b361ac164aa46feb31dda58794a6480`; those historical source paths and runtime rules are provenance, not the external app's current execution contract. Do not modify `gttome/Daily-AI-Brief`.

## 1. Required result

Create exactly six separate, article-specific, fact-grounded professional editorial images. Each must explain its article's mechanism, evidence, architecture, process, comparison or implication. Each final asset must be an exact **1200 × 630 PNG**, white or near-white, with safe margins, crisp technical linework, clear hierarchy and **mandatory readable explanatory text**.

The default is a detailed textbook/editorial mechanism illustration. **An infographic-like appearance is allowed when it is a better fit for the article**, including an annotated comparison, timeline, taxonomy, quantitative explanation, structured panel composition or a hybrid with dimensional illustration. The choice must follow the article, not a repeated house template. Infographic styling does not waive factual accuracy, text, explanatory depth, differentiation or file requirements. Two-dimensional clarity is legitimate; unnecessary machinery is not a quality requirement.

A title alone is insufficient. Generic AI art, sparse slides, icon boards, decorative dashboards and repeated boxes with swapped labels fail.

## 2. Current authority and conflict resolution

Apply the owner's current corrections: mandatory text; an app-independent GitHub exchange; and article-fit infographic styling. Use the current Compiler handoff for job and staging locations, and the prompt for the complete production instructions.

An empty legacy `visible_text_allowlist` or `labels_authorized=false` does not prohibit text. It means the app must complete a versioned, source-supported exact label specification. The owner authorizes drafting that wording through this task; do not falsely claim that the owner separately reviewed each label or accepted the resulting image. Keep the original job and bundle bytes/hashes immutable and record the correction separately.

The external app does not need the older production repository's executor, Work porter, Codex prohibition, fresh-chat proof, scheduler or historical repair-epoch architecture. The Compiler consumes the verified package, not those implementation details. Runtime checks that still require Work-session claims must be corrected as compatibility defects; never invent such claims.

The existing Compiler richness target remains **12 meaningful components**, despite the historical specification's lower eight-component floor. Use the stronger six-composition variation target below. No density target authorizes fabricated facts or decorative filler.

## 3. Article-to-image specification

Read the assigned complete article and relevant verified primary-source evidence before generation. Build one specification per story containing:

- One-sentence visual thesis: what this article actually explains or changes.
- At least two distinctive source-supported anchors.
- Input, transformation, resulting state/output, conditions and relevant evidence.
- Every component and arrow, with its role and source support.
- Separate verified facts from conceptual geometry, future plans and recommendations.
- Exact labels with placement targets, source support, units and qualifications.
- The selected composition and why it fits this article.
- Explicit exclusions for likely hallucinations or misleading interpretations.

Bind this review record to the edition, story ID, primary-source URL, job SHA-256 and recipe version. Keep identifiers, hashes, run history, unrelated articles and repository-control information out of generator-visible artwork instructions.

Trace every relationship correctly. Approval precedes the action it governs. Rejected evidence must not flow into a successful result without a supported recovery. A comparison is not returned material. Test results do not automatically rewrite a skill or train a model. Draw feedback only when supported.

Preserve numerical scope: a partial-transcript latency is not a full response time, an adoption count is not a productivity measurement, and a future target is not an achieved outcome. Label units and essential qualifications. Do not add product modes, capabilities, security guarantees, UI details or performance claims absent from the source.

## 4. Mandatory text

Prepare a **nonempty exact visible-text list** before generating. Start with roughly 6–9 useful short labels and add others when necessary for understanding. Each label should be unique, trimmed, at most eight words and at most 80 characters. Include needed headings, legends, units or qualifiers in this list; they are permitted when intentional and supported.

Render every required label clearly and exactly. Do not add unrelated words, unlisted digits, pseudo-writing, fake paragraphs, timestamps, command strings, filenames, hashes or metadata. A product or organization name may appear as accurate explanatory text when included in the list, without a logo or branded icon.

Use strong contrast, consistent type hierarchy, safe margins and unambiguous label-to-object association. No connector should cut through a label. Main labels must remain readable at the article's display size without zoom. Unused surfaces stay smooth and blank; do not simulate text with rows of short strokes.

Textless, title-only, missing-label, garbled-label and illegible-label images fail. OCR can assist review but cannot replace inspection of the actual saved pixels.

## 5. Explanatory quality

Retain the established target:

- At least 12 meaningful explanatory components across 3–5 major regions.
- Two visible internal substages in the dominant explanation.
- At least two supported secondary evidence, constraint, provenance or feedback relationships.
- Approximately 80–90% useful explanatory occupancy, with readable text and breathing room.
- A clear main reading path, subordinate evidence and refined supporting detail.
- Visible transformations or distinctions rather than labels applied to opaque containers.

For an infographic, components can be meaningful compared states, categories, timeline events, source-supported measures, annotations and evidence relationships. Internal substages can be a visible comparison or reasoning sequence. A connector does not automatically become an extra component; explain its actual role. Do not count repeated blank documents, bolts, icons, decorative tokens or colored boxes as density.

Use dimensional cutaways, layers, lattices, boundaries, state transitions or exposed assemblies where they improve explanation. An infographic may use more planar organization. Physicalized flows mean viewers can see what moves or changes; they do not require pipes, gears or an invented machine.

Use restrained professional accents and clear contrast. Color should reinforce grouping, state and relationships; also encode essential differences with labels, shapes, position or line treatment so meaning does not depend on color alone.

If the evidence cannot support the required richness, record the conflict before generation. Do not invent substeps, causal arrows, outcomes or feedback to meet a count.

## 6. Six-image variation

Reserve six story-appropriate visual signatures before the first generation. The operator can compare the plan and accepted images; each generator receives only its own story and assigned visual direction.

Generate, inspect and record one image at a time. Review article alignment, mechanism detail, text, palette and composition before the next image. Preserve accepted work and recover pending candidates before allocating duplicate attempts.

The set targets are:

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---:|
| Distinct composition signatures | 6 |
| Distinct layouts | At least 4 |
| Distinct diagram grammars | At least 4 |
| Distinct hierarchy patterns | At least 4 |
| Distinct annotation patterns | At least 3 |
| Distinct dominant palette families | At least 4 |
| Distinct mechanism metaphors | At least 4 |
| Images sharing one primary grammar | No more than 2 |
| Generic input–engine–output arrangements | No more than 2 |

Count differences visible in the pixels, not differently named fields. Maintain the common professional white-background standard, but vary composition, palette balance, perspective, object families, connectors and evidence representation. Infographic styling is one available choice, not a required quota or a reason to make all six infographic panels.

Record each actual signature with the story ID, image hash, composition, grammar, layout, palette, metaphor, object family, connectors, hierarchy, annotations, perspective, evidence treatment and supported feedback pattern. Never claim a feedback pattern when none exists.

Article truth has priority over novelty. If a planned diversity target conflicts with the articles, revise the plan using another truthful composition or record the unresolved constraint. Do not invent differences.

## 7. Prohibitions

- No people, faces, bodies, hands, avatars, person/group icons or humanoids. Represent human decisions through accurately labeled abstract decision points.
- No logos, branded icons, wordmarks or decorative branding.
- No photography, photorealistic scenes, cinematic backgrounds or desk/office props.
- No generic AI brains, robots, circuit heads or unrelated futuristic imagery.
- No invented product screenshots, terminal output, statistics, code or identifiers.
- No pseudo-text, placeholder writing, overlapping labels or clipping.
- No decorative machinery or unsupported security/verification badges.
- No cross-story or run-status/dashboard contamination.
- No low-quality substitute simply because generation or transport failed.

These reject weak execution, not an article-appropriate infographic, comparison or panel structure.

## 8. Review and persistence

Inspect the exact saved PNG against the article, source map and label list. Record separate judgments for subject match, factual scope, causal relationships, mechanism detail, readable text, contrast, hierarchy, composition, explanatory richness, no people, no overlap and differentiation. A self-description from the generator, correct dimensions, alt text, a hash or a successful build is not visual acceptance.

Provide substantive alt text describing the image's actual mechanism and main relationship. It complements, rather than replaces, visible text.

Verify PNG signature/structure, **1200 × 630 actual pixels**, 8-bit RGB or RGBA, noninterlaced encoding, byte count, SHA-256 and Git blob SHA-1. Finish any permitted export before review and locking; no stretching or clipping labels. Review the committed bytes again after GitHub readback.

Use the current authorized attempt bound, otherwise at most four generation attempts per story. Validation failures before generation and transport failures without generation consume no generation attempt. Wrong-subject contamination requires a fresh clean story-only request. Do not assume historical repair epochs allow extra attempts. Keep rejected candidates and honest defect records.

Accepted image bytes remain immutable. Transport or CI retries reuse them. A legitimate replacement needs an explicit new asset identity and review; do not silently overwrite history.

## 9. Single-story generator instruction

```text
Create exactly one final 1200 × 630 PNG for the attached single-story specification.
Use a white or near-white background, safe margins, crisp editorial linework,
clear hierarchy and rich source-supported explanation.

Explain this article's visual thesis, distinctive anchors and actual mechanism.
Use its assigned composition, palette and annotation plan. A professional
infographic-like or hybrid treatment is allowed when assigned because it fits
the article. Do not force an unnecessary machine metaphor.

Readable explanatory text is mandatory. Render every exact label from the
nonempty label specification, including required units and qualifications.
Render no additional characters or pseudo-writing. Keep labels readable at
the article's display size and clearly attached to the objects they explain.

Show only supported components and relationships. Keep data, action, evidence,
comparison and control connections distinct. Feedback requires source support.
Never invent facts, modes, metrics, guarantees, UI or causal arrows.

Use the established meaningful-detail criteria without decorative padding.
No people, humanoids, logos, photorealism, generic dashboards, repeated template
cards, fake paragraphs, clipping or overlapping labels.

Use only this story's content and visual direction. Do not import another
article's imagery, palette, labels, layout or operational history.
```

The operator must attach the completed story specification and exact labels. Template placeholders and an empty label list are not generation-ready inputs.
