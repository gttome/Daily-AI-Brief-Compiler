Summary: GitHub’s tutorial shows how to open a Copilot app session in VS Code while retaining its conversation, code changes and working context. The handoff lets an agent complete broad work remotely before a developer inspects the diff, continues the discussion, edits files and runs local tests. 4. An AI business record needs its origin trail

Extended recency fallbackSource article · about 8 min read

Originally published: 15 Oct 2026

Focus: Applied Generative AI for Knowledge Workers

Date: October 15, 2026

Topics: Record provenance, Legal holds, Risk-based retention

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Independent Reporting
Availability: Available

Summary: Reuters Legal commentary argues that consequential AI-generated business records may require more than the final document. Prompts, retrieved source versions, model identifiers, system instructions, intermediate outputs, human edits, approvals and triggered actions can determine whether a record is explainable or defensible. The author recommends proportional preservation, updated legal holds and vendor checks rather than retaining every interaction indefinitely.

Why it matters: Classify AI uses by consequence, then preserve the minimum origin package for high-risk records: inputs, versions, outputs, edits, approvals and actions. Test whether vendors can export that package before procurement, because rerunning a prompt cannot reconstruct historical context reliably.

5. Cheaper AI review needs a quality ledger

New developmentSource article · about 15 min read

Originally published: 19 Oct 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: October 19, 2026

Topics: Inference economics, Review quality, Context budgets

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Cloudflare cut average AI code-review cost from $2.43 to $0.77 and median latency from 4:09 to 1:32 by trimming duplicate context, reducing model calls, changing model and effort choices, and simplifying reviewer code. Blinded checks found important code-quality findings held roughly steady, while medium-effort security and performance reviewers still lost useful findings.

Why it matters: Treat token cost, latency and review quality as one evaluation. Replay identical repositories across model, effort and context changes; track acted-on findings and blinded importance ratings, then reject savings that merely suppress valuable security or performance feedback.

6. GRAFT routes bad behavior into removable parameters

New developmentSource article · about 13 min read

Originally published: 19 Oct 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: October 19, 2026

Topics: Reward hacking, Gradient routing, Training safeguards

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Anthropic-affiliated researchers propose GRAFT, which routes gradients from monitor-flagged behavior into a FORGET adapter removed after training instead of penalizing detected hacks. In a Qwen3-8B arithmetic environment, ablation nearly eliminated measured hacking while preserving gains. The study remains preliminary: monitors were simple, tasks small, and suitable anchor environments may be difficult to construct.

Why it matters: Replicate GRAFT only in a contained training study. Predefine held-out hack tests, compare penalties and prompting baselines, measure task loss after ablation, and document whether the monitor, anchor environment and model scale resemble the intended deployment.

Training signal boundaries — Compare retained and removed behavior on held-out hack fixtures.

Gradient routing treats monitor context as a selector for where learning lands rather than as the reward itself.
