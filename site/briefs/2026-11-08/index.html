1. BMO brings transaction evidence into contact-center calls

Recency fallbackSource article · about 4 min read

Originally published: 06 Nov 2026

Focus: Agents for Everyone

Date: November 6, 2026

Topics: Contact centers, Policy lookup, Employee review

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: BMO introduced an internal assistant for employees handling Canadian credit card calls. It matches transactions, retrieves policy guidance and prepares eligible requests for review. BMO says these inquiries currently take 12 to 14 minutes on average and targets a 20% to 30% reduction for eligible calls. Those figures are goals.

Why it matters: Service teams can map which facts an assistant gathers and which decisions an employee owns. Pilot a single request type, record handling time and correction rates, and route policy exceptions through the existing review process.

3. Microsoft shows how acceptance criteria can become software proofs

Recency fallbackSource article · about 16 min read

Originally published: 05 Nov 2026

Focus: Applied Generative AI for Knowledge Workers

Date: November 5, 2026

Topics: Acceptance criteria, Software verification, Proof boundaries

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Microsoft Research demonstrates how teams can turn acceptance criteria for agent-written software into formal specifications. Its example proves selected login behavior with Verus, then uses tests for boundaries the proofs do not cover. The approach leaves teams responsible for judging whether the written specification captures what users actually need.

Why it matters: Before accepting an agent-built tool, write down what must happen for valid inputs, missing information, and denied access. Ask engineering to map each requirement to proof or test evidence and keep uncovered cases visible.

4. Hightouch tests a second opinion for uncertain record matches

Extended recency fallbackSource article · about 10 min read

Originally published: 04 Nov 2026

Focus: Applied Generative AI for Knowledge Workers

Date: November 4, 2026

Topics: Record matching, Confidence calibration, Escalation thresholds

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Hightouch tested whether a smaller model could flag incorrectly merged customer identities and send uncertain cases to Claude Opus for a second opinion. One reported threshold sent 22% of cases to Opus and reached 98.8% accuracy, compared with 99.0% for Opus alone. The results come from one vendor benchmark.

Why it matters: Data stewards can test the pattern on a labeled sample before changing a live merge process. Track incorrect merges and missed duplicates separately, and require human review for cases where an error would affect customers.

Source: A Jev that can ask Opus for help gives Opus accuracy at 4x lower cost

5. Anthropic tests an offline pass for agent memory

Recency fallbackSource article · about 11 min read

Originally published: 06 Nov 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: November 6, 2026

Topics: Agent memory, Transcript review, Offline evaluation

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Anthropic reports that Claude can revisit session transcripts offline to correct memory notes and build reusable knowledge representations. In one internal wiki benchmark, scores rose from 54% to 61% as wikis grew, compared with 53% without a wiki. The tests use specific tasks and models, and the results come from Anthropic.

Why it matters: Test the memory pass on errors with known answers before expanding it. Compare the same tasks with and without rewritten notes, inspect changes against the original transcript, and track whether gains persist across sessions.

6. Inkling 2 Small opens for hosted fine-tuning

Recency fallbackSource article · about 14 min read

Originally published: 05 Nov 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: November 5, 2026

Topics: Hosted fine-tuning, Model evaluation, Mixture of experts

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Small for hosted inference and fine-tuning through Tinker. The mixture-of-experts model has 594 billion total parameters, 17 billion active parameters, and supports image inputs and adjustable effort. The lab plans a separate weights release after external safety testing, so downloadable deployment is still a future step.

Why it matters: Use the hosted release to compare quality, latency, and tuning behavior on a workload you already measure. Separate the results you can reproduce today from the deployment and licensing decisions that depend on the later weights release.
