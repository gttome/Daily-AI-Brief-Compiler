5. AlphaProof Code checks Signal machine code against human-approved claims

Recency fallbackSource article · about 6 min read

Originally published: 10 Nov 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: November 10, 2026

Topics: Machine-code proofs, Lean verification, Trusted assumptions

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Google DeepMind reports that AlphaProof Code agents generated machine-checkable Lean proofs for selected compiled Signal cryptography routines. Humans approved the specifications, while the system produced more than six million proof lines and caught seeded regressions. The result covers stated properties under trusted semantics and contracts; it does not establish that every implementation behavior or security assumption is correct.

Why it matters: Start with one critical routine and a human-reviewed specification. Record trusted libraries, compiler semantics, external contracts, seeded regressions, and review thresholds so a large proof corpus cannot be mistaken for proof of the whole product.

6. A knowledge graph restores answers only after retrieval is tuned

Extended recency fallbackSource article · about 7 min read

Originally published: 06 Nov 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: November 6, 2026

Topics: Numerical hallucination, Graph retrieval, Threshold tuning

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: EcoStatKG links ecological statistics from 6,871 Wikipedia articles and tests six models across eight retrieval strategies. Full-index graph retrieval nearly matched GraphRAG, but removing answer-bearing triples sharply reduced accuracy until a similarity threshold was tuned. Wrong retrieval caused most errors, while hallucination after correct injection stayed uncommon. The English-only benchmark and small clinical subset limit transfer.

Why it matters: Evaluate retrieval before blaming generation. Hold out answer-bearing records, measure wrong-retrieval errors separately, tune one threshold on known questions, and repeat on another domain before treating a graph or GraphRAG pipeline as generally reliable.
