6. Meta narrows the agent orchestrator to routing and handoffs

Recency fallbackSource article · about 12 min read

Originally published: 19 Oct 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: October 19, 2026

Topics: Thin orchestration, Context isolation, Typed handoffs

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Meta describes replacing a broad agent with a roughly 1,300-line orchestrator that routes requests to 14 context-isolated specialists across more than 20 workflows. The design uses deterministic routing, typed handoffs and narrow tool access. Meta reports much faster delivery and fewer edits, but the results come from its internal engineering environment.

Why it matters: Separate coordination from domain work when one agent’s context becomes noisy. Define explicit routes, minimal handoff fields and bounded tools, then compare completion quality and human revision rates before assuming that additional specialists improve reliability.

8. Agents for Everyone

RAG vs Fine-Tuning vs Prompt Engineering: Optimizing AI Models

Open the permanent video page
Channel: IBM Technology
Date: April 14, 2025
Duration: 13:10
Format: Video

Summary: Martin Keen compares three ways to improve AI responses: clearer prompting, retrieval-augmented generation and fine-tuning. The lesson distinguishes situations needing up-to-date or proprietary facts from those needing better instructions or more specialized model behavior. It explains the different infrastructure, latency and training implications; this is an evergreen methods lesson, not an independent vendor benchmark.

Why it matters: Diagnose the failure before selecting a tool. Start with clearer instructions, retrieve trusted current documents when knowledge is missing, and only consider fine-tuning when a measured specialization problem justifies its training cost.
