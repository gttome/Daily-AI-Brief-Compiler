1. Anthropic moves the Agent Skills standard into neutral Linux Foundation governance

Recency fallbackSource article · about 5 min read

Originally published: 22 Oct 2026

Focus: Agents for Everyone

Date: October 22, 2026

Topics: Agent Skills, Open governance, Workflow templates

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: Anthropic has donated the Agent Skills specification, reference SDKs and repositories to the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation. A Skill is a folder built around a plain-language SKILL.md file, with optional templates and references. Anthropic says more than 70 platforms support the format, while neutral governance will address versioning, security and interoperability.

Why it matters: You will learn a noncoding method for turning one repeated office workflow into a reviewed Skill: document instructions and outputs, test normal and adverse cases, assign ownership and a review date, then approve a version before reuse.

Summary: Microsoft and TK Elevator describe a mobile AI assistant for 25,000 service technicians maintaining 1.4 million units. Before a visit, it assembles tools, location details and likely issues from IoT data, manuals and notes. 5. AWS publishes a complete multi-turn reinforcement-learning pipeline for agents

New developmentSource article · about 12 min read

Originally published: 23 Oct 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: October 23, 2026

Topics: Multi-turn RL, Outcome rewards, SageMaker training

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: AWS shows a complete multi-turn reinforcement-learning pipeline for teaching GPT-OSS-20B to play Wordle on SageMaker AI. Its reported solve rate rose from 61% to 84% at default reasoning effort and from 15% to 69% at low effort, using outcome rewards, AgentCore execution and iterative rollout training.

Why it matters: You will learn how to frame an agent task, define outcome rewards, collect multi-turn rollouts and compare pretraining with post-training. The cost breakdown and failure modes help teams decide whether a bounded RL experiment is worth running.

6. IBM keeps interpretability hooks compatible with high-performance vLLM execution

Recency fallbackSource article · about 7 min read

Originally published: 21 Oct 2026

Focus: Technical AI Engineering

Date: October 21, 2026

Topics: Runtime interpretability, Graph replay, Inference overhead

Open the permanent story page

Evidence: Publisher Authored
Availability: Available

Summary: IBM Research introduces vLLM-Hook, a model-internals access layer that preserves vLLM graph replay while supporting activation inspection and steering. In publisher-run experiments, the team reports up to 20× lower instrumentation overhead than its predecessor, with roughly 2% overhead on small models and 6% on a 70-billion-parameter model.

Why it matters: You will learn where interpretability tooling can distort production inference and how graph-aware hooks reduce that penalty. The reported overhead ranges also provide a practical benchmark for deciding whether observability experiments belong in a serving-path evaluation.
